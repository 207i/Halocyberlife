01 / The Beginning

Before the simulations, there were drawings.

More than 13 years ago, I began exploring a compact rotating magnetic device as a personal engineering hobby.

I worked with geometry, a protractor, mechanical layouts, and ideas about how magnetic fields could interact with a small rotating assembly.

I wasn't trying to build a large machine. My ambition was miniaturization: a compact device that might eventually fit inside everyday electronics.

The original design included an unusually short shaft, a skateboard-style bearing, an actuator-controlled vertical movement mechanism, and a proposed three-capacitor electrical system.

I did not complete the experimental validation at that time. Today, I'm reviving the project to reconstruct the original design and investigate its performance.

Research history

04 / Electromagnetic Physics

What actually produces electrical power?

Electromagnetic induction occurs when magnetic flux through a conductor changes.

Faraday's law relates the induced voltage to the change in magnetic flux:

Vinduced = −N × dΦ/dt

Here, N is the coil-turn count and Φ is magnetic flux.

Rotating magnets relative to induction coils can generate voltage. The actual output depends on speed, magnetic geometry, flux linkage, coil resistance, and electrical loading.

Drawing electrical energy from the generator produces opposing torque under Lenz's law. Maintaining rotation under load requires a corresponding source of mechanical energy.

Engineering integrity

QIBS is a project name. The word "quantum" is not a claim of a demonstrated quantum effect. Permanent magnets alone are not assumed to supply unlimited energy. Continuous operation remains a testable objective, not a verified result.

05 / Mechanical Control

The lift-and-disengage concept

OPERATING MODE A

Rotor Engaged

The actuator positions the rotor within the intended magnetic and induction geometry. When externally driven, the rotor may induce electrical voltage in the stationary coils.

OPERATING MODE B

Rotor Disengaged

The actuator moves the assembly vertically, changing its magnetic coupling. A separate braking mechanism may be required to stop rotation fully and reliably.

06 / Electrical Architecture

The three-supercapacitor system

My original electrical concept used three capacitors with coordinated roles.

C1 / PRIMARY

Charging Bank

Receives energy from the generator through rectification and an appropriate charge-control circuit.

C2 / BUFFER

Load Supply

Supplies the regulated output stage while sufficient energy remains stored.

C3 / RESERVE

Overflow Management

Provides reserve capacity or captures available surplus energy within safe voltage and current limits.

→

Capacitor energy calculation

E = ½ × C × V²

E is energy in joules, C is capacitance in farads, and V is voltage.

A 100-farad supercapacitor charged to 2.7 volts stores approximately 364.5 joules.

Actual usable energy will be lower because of minimum operating voltage, leakage, converter losses, and other constraints.

Critical design principle

Capacitors store and transfer energy; switching between three capacitors does not create additional energy. The system must receive sufficient input energy to support its load and losses.

09 / Scientific Validation

The test that matters most: energy balance

A spinning rotor is not sufficient proof of electrical self-sufficiency.

We will measure every significant source and destination of energy.

ΔEstored = Einput − Eoutput − Elosses

The experimental plan includes:

  • Measuring rotor speed and coast-down time.
  • Measuring mechanical or electrical drive input.
  • Measuring coil voltage and current under load.
  • Measuring each capacitor's voltage over time.
  • Measuring regulated output power.
  • Recording actuator power consumption.
  • Calculating total energy delivered, stored, and lost.

The goal is to establish repeatable measurements that independent engineers can examine.
