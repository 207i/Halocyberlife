01 / The Beginning

Every invention begins with a question.

More than a decade ago, I began exploring ideas for compact energy technology as an independent engineering hobby.

The original concept grew from sketches, curiosity, mechanical design exploration, and a desire to imagine new possibilities for miniature electronic systems.

Today, QIBS is being revisited as a broader research initiative, with an emphasis on responsible engineering development, feasibility, and experimental evidence.

The goal is not simply to recreate an old drawing. It is to investigate what the original vision could inspire through future research.

04 / Engineering Perspective

Imagination needs scientific evidence.

Energy technology is governed by measurable physical principles. Any practical QIBS implementation would need to demonstrate where its operating energy comes from, how efficiently energy is converted, and how the complete system behaves under real conditions.

A compelling design illustration is not the same as a verified scientific result.

QIBS therefore approaches future performance claims as questions for engineering investigation rather than established outcomes.

Scientific integrity

"Quantum" is part of the QIBS project name; this page does not claim a demonstrated quantum effect. No perpetual-motion capability, energy creation, self-powered operation, or measured prototype performance is claimed.

08 / Scientific Validation

What would meaningful progress look like?

The goal of future QIBS research is to move from conceptual design toward evidence-based engineering conclusions.

An appropriate development program would include:

  • Defining research questions and measurable objectives.
  • Establishing engineering requirements and constraints.
  • Evaluating technical feasibility through suitable scientific methods.
  • Developing safe and repeatable experimental procedures.
  • Measuring relevant inputs, outputs, losses, and environmental conditions.
  • Comparing predicted behavior with observed results.
  • Documenting limitations, failed experiments, and design revisions.
  • Investigating practical applications only when supported by evidence.
Evidence comes first

The current public QIBS presentation is conceptual. It does not establish experimental validation, a functioning commercial device, or scientifically confirmed performance improvements.
