The Living Dome

What if the space above a swimming pool stopped being simply shelter and became a programmable environment?

Concept by Luis Amaya Halocyberlife Future Environments

There is a point where architecture stops being only shelter and begins becoming an interface.

Imagine a swimming pool beneath an enormous transparent half-dome — not a perfect hemisphere, but an intentionally offset structure that stretches, rises, and changes its geometry around the landscape.

During the day, it nearly disappears. At night, it comes alive.

Creative Note: This is an original speculative concept and creative direction by Luis Amaya / Halocyberlife. AI tools may be used during the visualization, editorial, and exploration process as part of the creative workflow. The concept is presented as artistic and architectural speculation rather than a claim that all illustrated technologies currently exist as one manufactured system.
01 · The Environment

A Transparent Architecture

The idea begins with a simple architectural gesture: place a swimming environment beneath a massive transparent shell.

The material might be glass, polymer, composite, membrane, or something that has not been invented yet.

For this exploration, the exact material is not the important part.

The question is what becomes possible when a transparent architectural surface is no longer passive.

02 · Solar Control

Controlling the Sun Without Losing the Sky

Imagine looking upward and still seeing an open blue sky, while different areas of the dome respond independently to sunlight.

Sections facing intense solar exposure could become darker or softer while the rest of the structure remains completely transparent.

As the sun moves across the sky, the response could move with it.

Instead of lowering a blind or closing the environment, the architecture itself changes its relationship with light.

What if the architecture above the pool became an artificial atmosphere?

03 · BIOGLOW™

Light Moving Through the Structure

At night, the architecture could become something completely different.

Imagine BIOGLOW™-inspired illumination traveling through structural ribs, edges, transparent surfaces, or embedded pathways throughout the dome.

Cyan light begins close to the water and gradually moves upward.

From outside, the dome might resemble a faint glowing organism resting over the landscape.

From inside, the water, architecture, illumination, reflections, and sky begin to merge.

04 · Digital Atmosphere

What If the Dome Became a Display?

Then comes another possibility: imagery.

Imagine floating in the pool while stars drift across the transparent enclosure overhead.

A nebula. An artificial sunset. Clouds. Aurora. Abstract light. Digital art.

At another moment, all of it disappears and the structure becomes transparent again, revealing the real night sky.

The dome shifts between architecture and canvas.

Environmental States

Architecture With Modes

Instead of remaining visually static, the enclosure could take on different identities throughout the day.

MODE 01

Clear

The shell becomes visually quiet, prioritizing the sky, landscape, and open-air feeling.

MODE 02

Solar

Selected regions respond to intense sunlight while surrounding areas remain transparent.

MODE 03

BIOGLOW

Structural elements and surfaces become softly luminous after sunset.

MODE 04

Dream

Imagery, projection, color, and movement transform the enclosure into an immersive visual environment.

MODE 05

Night Sky

Artificial effects disappear and the dome returns to transparency, framing the real stars overhead.

05 · Scale

Beyond the Backyard Pool

The same architectural idea could exist at very different scales.

A private residence might use a smaller asymmetric dome extending from the house over an indoor-outdoor pool.

A hotel could cover an entire courtyard.

A resort could place water, vegetation, restaurants, gathering spaces, and pathways beneath a huge transparent environment.

At sufficient scale, the dome stops feeling like a roof.

It becomes a controlled micro-environment beneath an artificial sky.

06 · Speculation

The Material Doesn't Have to Exist Yet

This concept is not dependent on one specific material.

It is not presented as a claim that every capability shown here can currently be manufactured as one system.

It is an architectural thought experiment.

Architecture normally begins by asking what can be built using the materials available today.

But the process can also be reversed.

Instead of asking,

“What can we build with the materials we have?”

ask:

“What would we build if the material behaved the way we wanted?”
